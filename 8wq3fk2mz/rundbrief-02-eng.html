News in July 2026

What's happened on heribert-berlin.de since mid-June

July 15, 2026

Dear readers,

right on time for the middle of the month, the newsletter is back. The premiere is slowly becoming a good habit, so this time the news comes at an easy pace: a new children's book, a new audiobook part, a fixed weekly rhythm on YouTube – and a website that's now easier to find your way around.

Waldemar the Owl: Volume 05 is here

The first children's book season has grown by one volume: "The Fox, His Family and the Birch" is out and available to order right now through Books on Demand. The matching video is waiting on YouTube as always – the link is ready on the children's book page. And the rhythm holds: things continue on the first day of every month – the next volume arrives on August 1. (The children's books are available in German.)

Audiobook: Part 3 is online

Kim Collins isn't taking a break either. Part 3 of the free audiobook for the novel "One Last Cà phê" is online – "Date at the Thai Place" – once again in German and in English. If you're just joining in or missed a part, you'll find everything gathered in the YouTube playlist, with subtitles in 13 languages. Part 4 is already announced: from July 30. And as always: if you'd rather not wait that long, reach for the book or the eBook – they already know the ending today.

New three times a week: Shorts on YouTube

New is a fixed beat for short videos on YouTube: Wednesdays for the children's books, Fridays for the Pakistan travel diary, and Sundays for the novel. Three dates, three worlds – one subscription to the channel is all it takes, and you won't miss any of them. And if you prefer to spend your time elsewhere: we've recently arrived on TikTok, Instagram and Facebook as well.

Little signposts on the website

Things have been happening around the edges, too. Every subpage now carries a little house symbol next to the language flags – one click, and you're back on the homepage. The most important buttons no longer sit only at the bottom of the page but right up top under the heading; no more endless scrolling on your phone. And the Pakistan pages now lead straight to the complete video playlist on YouTube – so the travel diary can now be watched as well as read.

And what's next?

The sequel, "One More Cà phê," is being written. I won't reveal more just yet – a good Cà phê needs its time, after all.

Thank you for being part of this. Until next time!
Yours, Heribert Lange

And now, allow me to show the boss in …

May I introduce:
Eduard "Eddi" von Duckstein

A letter to my readers

Dear readers,

if you've read my letter about Kim, you already know him by hearsay: the boss who models himself on Sherlock Holmes. Today he gets his own entrance. Eduard von Duckstein, 37, known to everyone simply as Eddi, head of the small agency where my novel makes its home.

Eddi's office says it all, really. On the desk lies a pipe, although he doesn't smoke; his idol did, after all. Next to it an old globe, on the wall books of which, as Kim suspects, he has read very few – and the only picture hanging there, neatly framed, is the street map of Berlin. Eddi grew up with Holmes and Watson and feels called to higher things. Kim certifies that he would have cut a fine figure at Vienna's Burgtheater, and that is at most half in jest: when Eddi puts on a disguise or stages a scene, every performance lands. Only when two new cases flutter onto his desk and he generously lets Kim choose which one he'd like to take does he have to ask, sheepishly, afterwards, which one the second one actually was.

His first big entrance in the novel, however, belongs to his phone. Eddi is chewing Kim out for not answering his calls twenty times, holds up his own phone as proof – and at exactly that moment it rings out from it, through the entire office: "Little Curly-Tail, why do you keep calling me? You know I'm at the hairdresser's and can't pick up!"

Which brings us to his greatest weakness: Eddi is permanently in love. This time, however – and this is new – the love is apparently returned. Her name is Emona, his "Pearl of the Adriatic." He buys roses for his rose, fails to notice a new hairstyle, but has learned to admire it extensively anyway, just to be safe.

Don't underestimate him for it. When things get serious, Eddi holds the shop together, plans, assigns roles and keeps the overview – and his theatrical blood will prove very useful in this novel. So should you ever come across a road worker in Berlin who spends a remarkably long time tinkering with a street lamp: give him my regards.

Yours, Heribert Lange
