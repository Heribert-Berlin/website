What has happened on heribert-berlin.de since mid-August

September 15, 2026

Dear readers,

in the last newsletter I told you that I am putting the time freed up from the audiobook into reading, writing and talking with fellow authors. Four weeks later, you can see that on my website. But Waldemar comes first.

Waldemar the Owl: Volume 07 is here

On September 1st „The Bat, Her Family and the Linden Tree" was published. Jan sleeps upside down under the roof beam of an old barn, together with his wife Lena and their three young ones Ella, Emil and Enno. Their day begins when the sun goes down. Along the way children learn that bats are not birds but mammals, that they fly with their hands and see in the dark with loud calls. And the ancient linden tree next to the barn accompanies the family through the whole year. As always, the matching video waits on my children's channel, and volume 07 is now also available as an e-book, like volumes 04 to 06, at 9.99 euros. Volume 08, „The Great Tit, Its Family and the Larch", follows on October 1st. (The children's books are published in German.)

My reviews have a home of their own

Until now all my reviews stood together on one page, and with every book that became harder to read. Now there is an overview with the book covers, and every review has its own page. Since the last newsletter three have been added: „Das Lebenshaus, Band 1" by Frau A., „Daniel, Christopher, Barcelona" by Leon Herbertz, a quiet love story between an architect and a sports teacher, and „Der Tanz der Mutterliebe", also by Frau A. The reviews themselves are written in German.

With the last book something happened that moved me deeply. Astrid Hecken, who writes as FRAU A., replied to my review, publicly and very openly: why she wrote this book, for whom, and what dance and music meant to her. With her permission her reply now stands directly beneath my review. One sentence from it has stayed with me: „You saw the way." You will find both on my review page.

Reader voices: what others write about my books

Also new is a page of reader voices. It collects reviews that readers have written on Amazon about „One Last Cà phê" and my Pakistan book, unchanged and in their own words. One of them, by the way, comes from Frau A. And so the circle closes.

New in the game room: Waldemar Mahjong

For you as a newsletter reader there is something to play again. The game room now has a fourth game: Waldemar Mahjong. Instead of characters and bamboo, the animals from Waldemar's forest are on the tiles. Whoever finds two identical tiles lying free may clear them away. To start with there are 40 tiles, and anyone who wants more takes twice as many. Simon, the narrator of my children's audiobooks, reads the instructions aloud in German, English and Chinese, and the time limit that protects children is built in here too.

And Waldemar Memory has learned something new: with every pair they find, children now hear what the animal is called, in German, English or Chinese. That way you learn in passing that the hedgehog is called Igel in German.

And as always: pass it on

If you enjoy this newsletter, please feel free to forward it. Anyone can sign up on the sign-up page. And if you would like to tell me something, simply reply to this email, I read every reply.

Thank you for being here. Until next time!
Yours, Heribert Lange

And now we fetch someone up from the basement …

May I introduce our Q:
Dr. Wulf-Dieter Kluge

A letter to my readers

Dear readers,

on the brass plate at the agency door there are three names: von Duckstein, Collins & Kluge. You already know two of them. The third is rarely seen, and there is a simple reason for that: Dr. Wulf-Dieter Kluge works in the basement.

Kim assigned the roles in his agency after the Bond films long ago. He himself is James Bond, Eddi is M, Olga is Miss Moneypenny. And Dr. Kluge, 55 years old, is Q, the man who builds the marvels.

He is introduced by Eddi, in a way only Eddi can manage. „He builds everything, even things that don't exist yet", he says, and right after that: „He is like a spider in its web, waiting for prey, because sadly one thing is completely missing in our dear Dr. Wulf-Dieter Kluge: ideas of his own. Otherwise he would surely have left the company long ago to devote himself to polishing his Nobel Prizes." That is Eddi's way of praising someone to the skies. And then he admits: „How he does it, none of us knows."

Dr. Kluge builds the cameras with transmitter, receiver and recording equipment practically overnight. And because Eddi points out that nobody wants to watch surveillance videos around the clock, he quickly writes a program to go with them, one he had forgotten to write. It starts recording as soon as anything moves that is bigger than a squirrel. Everything is ready before midnight.

In meetings he sits silently in the corner and seems not to be listening at all, until he suddenly nods remarkably hard. Or bursts out: „Total nonsense! You're not seeing the obvious!" And then he is right.

My favourite thing about him is a small detail: when a new camera works, he straightens his great-aunt's sleeve protectors and claps his hands with delight. What makes others marvel, he calls „child's play".

He rarely comes upstairs. When one Monday morning he actually rides the lift up to the third floor, Kim records it like a moon landing: „A small step for the world, an epoch-making one for him, from eternal darkness into the light of this radiant autumn day." And when the others step into the limelight after the work is done, he pleads tiredness. He does not seek the public eye.

So you might think you know him now: the quiet, friendly tinkerer from the basement, bursting with knowledge, someone you can talk to for hours. But at some point in the novel someone asks, quite directly, the question nobody had dared to ask out loud: how does Dr. Kluge actually manage all of this?

The answer is in the book, and I am not giving it away here. Only this much: there is considerably more behind Dr. Kluge than a basement room would suggest.

With that, the agency is complete. Who gets the next letter, I am not telling yet either.

Yours, Heribert Lange
