News in June 2026

What's happened on heribert-berlin.de since the end of May

June 15, 2026

Dear readers,

May is barely over, and there's already plenty to tell. The short version: two new releases, a refreshed website, and a promise for the future. The slightly longer version follows here.

More children's books: Season 1, Volume 04

Anyone who has stopped by the homepage in recent days could hardly have missed it: a green star is bouncing there, announcing the news. Volume 04 of the first children's book season is out and available to order right now through Books on Demand. The matching video is waiting on YouTube as always – the link is ready on the children's book page.

Kim Collins for your ears, now in English too

The audiobook for the novel "One Last Cà phê" is growing as well: Part 2 is out – and in two versions at once, in German and in English. The audiobook stays free and is released piece by piece; around 36 parts are planned. So feel free to get just a little addicted. And if the suspense gets to be too much while you're out and about, you don't have to wait for the final part: the book and the eBook already know the ending today.

Behind the scenes of the website

More has changed around the edges than meets the eye. The author page was rewritten from scratch and translated into all three languages of the site; if you'd like to know why Vietnam became my second home, you'll find the answer there. The Pakistan pages now lead, with a golden shimmer, straight to the trailer and the books; quite a few images were shrunk so everything loads faster; and the sign-up form for this very newsletter is finally visible in full, with no scrolling at all. So if you know someone who's still missing here, spreading the word just got even easier.

On the subject of privacy, there's a piece of news that sounds almost exotic these days: this website uses no cookies and no tracking. No one is reading over your shoulder. Since the beginning of June, that's stated in black and white in the privacy policy as well.

And what's next?

The new author page hides a hint that attentive readers shouldn't skip past: the sequel to the novel already has a name. It will be called "One More Cà phê." I won't reveal more just yet – a good Cà phê needs its time, after all.

Thank you for being part of this. Until next time!
Yours, Heribert Lange

May I introduce:
Kim Collins

A letter to my readers

Dear readers,

before my novel "One Last Cà phê" makes its way into your hands, I'd like to introduce the character with whom it all begins: my narrator, Kim Collins.

Kim is 23, born as Kim Nguyễn in Mũi Né on the Vietnamese coast, where his mother runs a small family café. He grew up in Berlin, and to this day he lives between these two worlds. He owes the surname Collins to his American adoptive father; how that came about, the novel tells better than I could here. The title, by the way, comes from Kim himself: a cà phê sữa đá – coffee with sweet condensed milk over ice – is the last thing he drinks before he has to leave Vietnam yet again.

In Berlin, Kim works as a detective. The agency is housed in a rented three-room apartment, and no one there is getting rich. No one needs to, either – they work for the love of it. His boss models himself on Sherlock Holmes, Olga at the front desk grew up on TV crime dramas, and Kim himself? "I was always James Bond," he says, "I just never had his luck with women." Around the office everyone calls him Casey, because at some point his initials K. C. turned into the name of the "sleeping prophet" Edgar Cayce.

Kim observes closely, asks questions faster than the person across from him can think, and stays calm long after others have started to sweat. At the same time, he brings Olga flowers and holds their standing Monday date at the Thai place around the corner sacred. In only one thing is this man, who otherwise misses nothing, astonishingly blind: his own feelings. What he discovers after his vacation, while sorting through his pictures, throws him off course more than any case ever could. I won't reveal more here.

Speaking of cases: two of them land on his desk right at the start. A suspected affair and an abduction in which something is off from the very first page. Where it all leads, no one at the agency suspects. Kim least of all.

Come along with him for a while. It's worth it.
Yours, Heribert Lange
