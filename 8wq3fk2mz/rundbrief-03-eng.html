What has happened on heribert-berlin.de since mid-July

August 15, 2026

Dear readers,

this time the newsletter carries a decision along with the news, one I thought about for quite a while. But first things first, the pleasant things come first.

Waldemar the Owl: Volume 06 is here

On August 1st „The Deer, His Family and the Maple Tree" was published, the sixth volume of the first season. Tom, Frieda, Kai and Kira accompany a maple tree through the seasons, and as always the matching video waits on my children's channel. The rhythm stays the same: it continues on the first day of every month. Volume 07, „The Bat, Her Family and the Linden Tree", arrives on September 1st. (The children's books are published in German.)

New: Waldemar is now available as an e-book

That is a first for my website. Volumes 04, 05 and 06 are now also available as e-books from Books on Demand, at 9.99 euros each. If you read aloud from a tablet in the evening, or would like to carry the stories with you, you now have the choice. This is not meant to replace the printed books, no screen replaces a picture book you can hold and turn the pages of. But it is good to have both.

Audiobook: Part 4 is online, and then it takes a break

Part 4 of the free audiobook for „One Last Cà phê" is called „From Words to Action". It has been online since July 30th, the English version since July 31st. And for now it is the last one. Part 5 is written and planned, but it will not appear in August. The audiobook is going on a creative break. I would rather tell you why than leave you guessing.

Why the audiobook is pausing

An audiobook episode is not simply me reading into a microphone, it is a small film project: preparing the text, generating the voices, creating the images, editing, placing the subtitles into the timeline by hand, plus descriptions and thumbnails. That is many days of work for a single episode.

I took a calm look at what actually arrives, and I learned two things. First: the short promotional videos do get watched. Second: that attention stays with the short videos. It does not carry on to the audiobook, and even more rarely to the book. So far the episodes themselves reach very few ears.

So I am setting the audiobook aside for now, with no fixed end date. This is neither a farewell nor a sulk, it is a sober calculation: I have a limited amount of time, and I want to give it to where it does more good.

The shorts continue exactly as before, they are already scheduled on YouTube through November. And all four parts stay online, in German and in English, with subtitles in 13 languages. Anyone who would like to start listening can do so at any time.

And if you want to know how things turn out for Kim and Nong, there is nothing to wait for: the book and the e-book already know the ending.

What I do with the time I gain

Read. Write. And talk with fellow authors.

That has already begun. I now belong to a small group of authors where we read each other, give honest feedback and beat the drum for one another. Stefan Röser, whom you know from my Reading Tips, is part of it.

Most recently I read the new book by Nastassja Böcher, before its release. My review is finished and waiting in the drawer, it will go onto my review page as soon as the book is available. Lying open beside it is „Der Tanz der Mutterliebe" by Astrid Hecken, who writes as FRAU A. Both of them read this newsletter, by the way, so a warm hello from here.

We self-publishers have no publishing house behind us, so we help each other, and that costs nothing but time and a bit of attention. Which is exactly what I have now.

And the writing continues too. „One More Cà phê" is growing. Kim and Nong have long since arrived in Berlin, but that does not mean Nong is allowed to stay: for family reunification he needs the B1 language certificate. So he is learning German. How that turns out I am not telling yet.

And now it is your turn

This newsletter goes to a small and very personal circle. That is not a complaint, quite the opposite: with almost every one of you I know who is reading. Which is exactly why I am asking you and not some statistic.

What do you like about this newsletter, what bores you, what is missing? And how do you get on with the website: do you find what you are looking for, or do you click yourself tired? Even one sentence helps me, and an honest sentence helps more than a kind one.

Simply reply to this email, it comes straight to me, and I answer every one.

And if you know someone who might read Waldemar aloud, who enjoys crime stories or who is simply curious: please forward this newsletter. Anyone can sign up on the sign-up page. Word of mouth is the only advertising that really works for me.

Thank you for being here. Until next time!
Yours, Heribert Lange

And now in comes the woman who already knows what you need …

May I introduce our Miss Moneypenny:
Olga Lenz

A letter to my readers

Dear readers,

Miss Moneypenny is not my idea, it is Kim's own image. When he thinks about the agency, he thinks in Bond films: himself as James Bond, Dr. Kluge as Q, Eddi as M. And in the first room sits Olga Lenz, 38 years old, whom he simply calls „the pearl of the office".

She is also the first to notice him when he returns from Vietnam after weeks away: „Hello Cayce, you're back!" His name is not Cayce, of course, it is Kim Collins, but everyone pronounces his initials KC that way. Kim bought her a bunch of flowers on the way in, and you can already guess why.

Olga presides over an enormous desk in the first room, the one you burst into when you enter the agency. Reception, switchboard, coordination, research, all of it runs through her, usually at the same time. She writes the emails Kim tosses at her in three keywords, and Eddi's as well. She makes the tea the British way, just as Kim likes it: Earl Grey with a little sugar and cream. And when Eddi is in love again and good for nothing, she simply holds the place together on her own.

She would not have to. Olga is three times a widow and financially independent, she could stop tomorrow. She still comes in every day, because she has loved crime stories since childhood. The novel explains it in a single sentence: „Olga was the Mimi type, yes, exactly that one: Mimi never goes to bed without a crime story." The agency is not a job for her. It is the crime story she finally gets to be part of.

Her great moment she owes, of all things, to a cup of coffee. Eddi has just staged one of his scenes, Olga bursts out laughing into her cup, the coffee sprays across the desk and hits a few papers. She dabs the sheets dry, pauses, stops: on one of the letters the word ‚HI' has suddenly appeared where nothing stood before. At that point most people would have wiped on. Not Olga. She thinks to herself that if something is written there which does not belong, there might be more of it, and she brings out the rest. When she later pushes the letter across the table to Kim, it reads ‚HELP' in large letters, written in lemon juice by someone who was hoping for exactly this kind of reader. Her own comment, with a smile but entirely in earnest: she was right, as always.

And by then she had done far more than wipe. Before Kim can even sit down, she has read the whole file, printed maps of the addresses and found out who the missing woman is. That is how Olga works: quietly, thoroughly and always one step ahead of everyone else.

My favourite thing about her, though, are her Mondays. For many months now she has been sitting with Kim at Húng's place every Monday at eight in the evening, and on that day Húng opens almost only for the two of them. It is rarely about work and almost always about life. She orders the rosé they both enjoy and the wonton soup to warm up. She is the first person Kim told about Nong.

At the end of the novel everyone has their outlook. Eddi has Emona, Kim has Nong. About Olga it says only: „how things go on for Olga is still written in the stars …" For now she has next Monday.

I know what I have planned for her. Naturally I am not telling you here.

Yours, Heribert Lange
